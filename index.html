Undergraduate Researcher · Neuroimaging · Machine Learning · NeuroAI

I am a third-year undergraduate student at Woxsen University, Hyderabad, reading Computer Science with a specialisation in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning. My research sits at the intersection of deep learning and neuroimaging — specifically, how self-supervised and contrastive methods can be made clinically meaningful for the study of neurodegenerative disease.

I currently work as a research intern at IIT Delhi under Dr. Tapan Gandhi, developing clinically-guided contrastive pretraining frameworks for structural MRI analysis. More broadly, I am drawn to questions about what representation learning can reveal about brain structure — and how to close the gap between statistical prediction and biological understanding.

Research Alignment Platform active

An agentic research environment that synchronizes code implementation, domain standards, and paper writing (.tex) within a single platform. Built for AI/ML/DL researchers. Powered by Claude Code. Researchers move from idea to reproducible result without leaving the environment.

GitHub

Kensho — Agentic AI for PTSD Trigger Identification active

A multi-agent system that analyzes dream descriptions against DSM-5/ICD-11 clinical guidelines to detect hidden PTSD indicators. Semantic-parsing, trigger-detection, and automated reporting modules convert subjective dream content into structured clinical insights. Third runner-up, INNOVERSE hackathon.

GitHub

ADNI sMRI Alzheimer's Detection with 3D CNNs active

A 3D-CNN classification pipeline for structural MRI data from ADNI (430 CN, 250 MCI subjects). Full preprocessing workflow — skull stripping, affine MNI normalization, z-score intensity scaling — followed by training of DenseNet-121 and ResNet variants via MONAI.

GitHub

EEG Signal Decomposition for Schizophrenia Classification active

Comparative study of six decomposition techniques — DWT, CWT, WPD, Hilbert Transform, FFT, EMD — for EEG-based schizophrenia classification on the Poland Schizophrenia Dataset. Best accuracy of 99.32% with Hilbert Transform features. Also explored Spiking Neural Networks for time-series modeling.
